The city
that grew
upward

Wedged into a river gorge between two mountain walls, Nova Kaelen stacks instead of spreading: quays at the water, four expressway decks above them, concrete housing tiers above those, white-glass towers on sixty-year-old podiums at the top. Four levels of traffic can cross over your head at once. You come for the vertical strangeness of it. You stay for the food on the stairs.

COASTAL POCKET

Vesperia occupies a rugged coastal pocket of the Pacific Northwest, wedged between the western edge of Washington State, the southern border of British Columbia and the open swells of the North Pacific. It is small, dense and mountainous: a corridor rather than a continent.

That position is the whole story. Snow-capped ridges and old-growth pine drop straight into deep-water ports on the lanes between North America and East Asia, so you can stand on a container quay and be on a mountain trail forty minutes later.

    Kaelic is a port creole: English grammar, Latin left by the customs clerks, French left by the shipping houses. Twelve words cover a whole visit, and everything else can be said in English.

    [10] Know
    before you go

    HONEST NOTES,
    NOT WARNINGS
    You are on camera

    One of the most monitored cities on the coast, with biometric gates on transit and camera panels on every tier. Visitors are rarely bothered by it. Carry your entry document and expect face checks at station barriers.

    Two quarters to skip

    The eastern tenement blocks and the industrial yards behind Power Node 4 are closed to visitors and policed unpredictably. There is nothing there for a traveller, whatever older guidebooks claim.

    Power cuts happen

    Short outages hit the eastern tiers most weeks, usually under twenty minutes. Lifts stop, stair lighting stays on. Keep a torch, and do not be in a tower lift at 21:00.

    Manners on the stairs

    Stairways are streets and also front rooms. Climb on the right, ask before photographing a kitchen, pay in cash. Tipping is not expected. Ordering a second dish is.

    Air on hazy days

    Industrial haze settles in the gorge in high summer. Upper tiers are always clearer than the quays, which is a good reason to plan mornings low and evenings high.

    Three perfect days

    Day one: quays, the Stacks, night market. Day two: Upper Spine bridges, cable car, Skyline Terrace at sunset. Day three: Old Customs Quarter, archive dome, then Pine Ridge Park to end quietly.

    This atlas was built
    by the people who live here

    There is no tourism ministry behind this. Vesperia has a Bureau of Visitor Revenue, and it publishes figures: arrivals, spend per head, hotel occupancy on the upper tiers. It does not publish a map of the stairs, or opening hours for a kitchen on the Lantern Slope, or the fact that the archive's lower vault has been sealed since 1998.

    So the rest of us wrote it. Photographs from residents of Halstead and Lantern Slope, timetables checked on foot, prices confirmed the week of publication, corrections taken from anyone who finds one. The state counts what the city earns. The city keeps count of everything else.
